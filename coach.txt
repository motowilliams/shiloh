I would built this program around gradual lean-mass gain, adequate fueling for training and growth, and increased strength/power, rather than simply trying to make you gain weight.

For adolescent athletes, the AAP recommends gradual weight gain and notes that simply adding lots of calories or protein can increase fat more than muscle. It also recommends supervised strength training with gradual load increases and good technique.  Female athletes also need particular attention to adequate energy availability, iron, calcium, vitamin D, etc., because inadequate fueling can contribute to RED-S, poorer performance, and bone injuries.

Starting targets:

* Weight gain: roughly 0.25–0.75 lb/week is a reasonable performance-oriented starting target.
* Calories: begin around your normal maintenance intake plus 300–400 calories/day, then adjust based on a 2–3 week weight trend, recovery, hunger and performance. The AAP specifically recommends adding about 300–400 calories/day for young athletes trying to gain lean mass.
* Meals: 3 substantial meals + 2–3 snacks daily.
* Protein: include a meaningful protein source at every meal/snack rather than relying on protein supplements.
* Carbohydrates: don’t make this a low-carb “clean bulk.” Discus requires explosive training, and carbohydrate is important fuel for high-intensity work.
* Fats: include plenty of nutrient-dense fats to make gaining calories easier.
* Hydration: water throughout the day, with electrolytes when training heavily or in hot conditions.
* Sleep: ideally 8–10 hours/night for an athlete her age.

7-day nutrition + throwing/lifting framework

Healthy Weight-Gain, Nutrition & Strength Program

Primary Goals:

1. Gradually increase body mass.
2. Maximize lean muscle gain rather than simply gaining body fat.
3. Increase lower-body and upper-body strength.
4. Develop explosive power and rotational strength.
5. Improve throwing-specific positions and force production.
6. Maintain adequate energy availability for normal growth.
7. Arrive at competitions fueled, hydrated and recovered.

PART 1 — DAILY NUTRITION

Daily Meal Structure

Aim for:

Breakfast → Snack → Lunch → Pre-workout snack → Post-workout meal/snack → Dinner → Evening snack

You do not need to eat perfectly every day. Consistency over months is much more important.

Breakfast

Choose:

* 3 eggs
* 2 slices whole-grain toast
* 1–2 tablespoons peanut or almond butter
* Oatmeal with berries and milk
* Greek yogurt

OR

* Oatmeal made with milk
* Banana
* Peanut butter
* 2 eggs
* Greek yogurt

Mid-Morning Snack

Choose one:

* Greek yogurt + granola + berries
* Peanut-butter sandwich + milk
* Trail mix + fruit
* Cheese + whole-grain crackers + fruit
* Smoothie

Lunch

Build lunch around:

Protein + carbohydrate + fruit/vegetable + healthy fat

Example:

* 4–6 oz chicken
* 1.5–2 cups rice
* Black beans
* Avocado
* Salsa
* Fruit
* Milk

Other options:

* Turkey sandwich + cheese
* Chicken burrito bowl
* Beef and rice bowl
* Pasta with meat sauce
* Chicken quesadilla + rice
* Hamburger + potatoes + fruit

Pre-Workout Meal/Snack

Approximately 1–3 hours before training:

* Banana + peanut butter
* Bagel + eggs
* Greek yogurt + granola
* Turkey sandwich
* Oatmeal + fruit
* Cereal + milk
* PB&J + milk

Prioritize carbohydrates and moderate protein.

Avoid making the pre-workout meal extremely high in fat or fiber immediately before training.

Post-Workout

Within roughly 1–2 hours:

Protein + carbohydrate

Examples:

* Chocolate milk + banana
* Chicken + rice
* Greek yogurt + granola + fruit
* Turkey sandwich + fruit
* Beef + potatoes
* Eggs + toast + fruit

Dinner

Example:

* 5–7 oz lean beef, chicken, turkey or pork
* 1.5–2 cups potatoes, rice or pasta
* Vegetable
* Fruit
* Avocado or olive oil
* Milk

Before Bed

A calorie-dense evening snack can be very useful for an athlete who struggles to gain weight.

Options:

* Greek yogurt + granola + honey
* Cottage cheese + fruit
* Peanut-butter toast + milk
* Cereal + milk + banana
* Smoothie
* Oatmeal + peanut butter

PART 6 — PROGRESSION

Do not chase maximum lifts.

The goal is to become stronger while maintaining explosive movement.

Main lifts

When you complete every prescribed set with excellent technique and still has approximately 2 good repetitions available:

Increase the load slightly the following week.

Typical increases:

Upper body: +2.5–5 lb

Lower body: +5–10 lb

If technique breaks down, don’t increase the weight.

⸻

PART 7 — 4-WEEK TRAINING CYCLE

Week 1

Establish technique.

Moderate weights.

RPE approximately 6–7.

Week 2

Add a small amount of weight where appropriate.

RPE approximately 7.

Week 3

Small additional increase.

RPE approximately 7–8.

Week 4

Deload.

Reduce lifting volume by approximately 30–40%.

Keep some explosive work but reduce fatigue.

Then begin another cycle.
